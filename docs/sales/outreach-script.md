# Design-partner outreach script

**Goal:** book a 20-minute discovery call. We aren't selling yet. The product
doesn't exist, so be upfront about that. The ask is "help shape it, and get it
free while we build it."

**The pilot offer:** 3 shops get Answer Truck free for 90 days, then a locked-in
founder price. In return the shop does a 20-minute call now, gives feedback every
couple of weeks during the pilot, and gives a testimonial if it works.

**Who to reach:** the owner. Call mid-morning on Tuesday–Thursday, when techs are
out on jobs and the office is quieter. Avoid Monday mornings and the first cold
snap or heat wave, when shops are slammed.

Two angles are below. Lead with whichever fits the shop, and test both: alternate
them across the list and track which one books more calls in `prospects.md`.

---

## Angle A — "Never miss an after-hours emergency"

Best for: shops that advertise 24/7 or emergency service, drain/sewer shops, and
HVAC shops heading into winter.

### Cold call (about 30 seconds)

> Hi, is this [owner]? This is Nathan, I'm local, in [city]. I'll be quick.
> I'm building an AI receptionist just for HVAC and plumbing shops. It answers
> after hours, figures out whether it's a real emergency (no heat, water on the
> floor) and gets you or your on-call tech the details right away, instead of
> leaving it on voicemail.
>
> I'm not selling anything yet. I'm looking for three Twin Cities shops to help
> shape it, and they'd get it free for 90 days. Could I get 20 minutes to ask
> how you handle after-hours calls today?

**If yes:** book the time and confirm it by text.
**If "we have an answering service":** "Good, that's exactly who I want to talk
to. What do you like about it, and what drives you nuts?" This is still a
discovery call.
**If "we just forward to my cell":** "How often does that wake you up for
something that could've waited until morning?" Let them talk.
**If "not interested":** "No problem. Mind if I check back in spring?" Log it as `pass`.

### Voicemail (under 20 seconds)

> Hi [owner], Nathan here, local to the Twin Cities. I'm building an after-hours
> AI receptionist for HVAC and plumbing shops and looking for three local shops
> to try it free. I'd love 20 minutes of your take. [number]. Again, [number].

## Angle B — "Missed calls are jobs going to the next shop"

Best for: busy shops with lots of reviews, owner-operators who are on jobs all
day, and shops doing replacements or installs (big tickets).

### Cold call (about 30 seconds)

> Hi, is this [owner]? This is Nathan, I'm local, in [city]. Quick one.
> When you're on a job and the phone rings, what happens to that call?
>
> *(let them answer)*
>
> That's what I'm working on. It's an AI receptionist built for HVAC and plumbing
> shops. It picks up every call, books the job or takes the details, and texts you
> a summary, so the homeowner doesn't hang up and call the next shop on Google.
>
> I'm looking for three local shops to help build it, and they'd get it free for
> 90 days. Could I get 20 minutes to hear how calls are handled at your shop?

**If "my wife/office manager handles it":** "Great. Could I talk to both of you?
I'd want to know where it gets hard for them too."
**If "we don't miss many calls":** "Do you know how many go to voicemail in a
week? A lot of shops I talk to don't track it." This plants the question without
arguing.

## Follow-up cadence (both angles)

| Day | Touch |
|-----|-------|
| 1 | Call. If no answer, leave a voicemail and send the email the same day. |
| 3 | Text: "Hi [owner], Nathan from Answer Truck. Left you a VM about the free receptionist pilot for local HVAC/plumbing shops. Worth a 20-min chat?" |
| 7 | Second call, no voicemail. |
| 12 | Short email reply on the same thread: "Still looking for 1–2 more shops for the pilot. Should I close the loop?" |
| After that | Mark `pass`, revisit in spring. |

Text only after a call or email has introduced you, and stop right away if they
say no.

---

## Discovery call questions (20 minutes)

Write down their exact words, because they become website copy later.

**How calls work today**
1. Walk me through what happens when the phone rings during the day, and after hours.
2. Who answers? Office staff, you, an answering service, voicemail?
3. Roughly how many calls a week, and how many do you think get missed?
4. What does a missed call cost you? Have you lost a job you know about?

**After hours and emergencies**
5. What counts as a real emergency for you, and what can wait till morning?
6. Who's on call, and how do they want to be reached (call, text, app)?
7. What do you charge for after-hours visits? Do callers ask about the price upfront?

**Booking and tools**
8. What do you schedule in (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, paper, Google Calendar)?
9. Should a receptionist book straight onto the calendar, or take details for you to call back?
10. What do callers ask most often (pricing, availability, service area, "do you work on X brand")?

**Fit**
11. Have you tried an answering service or AI tool before? What happened?
12. What would it have to do for you to trust it with your customers?
13. If it worked, what would it be worth per month?
14. Would you be up for a free 90-day pilot once it's ready?

**Close:** "Thanks, this helps a lot. I'll follow up by [date] with what I'm
building based on this."
